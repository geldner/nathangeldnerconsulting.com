Economic Modeling

Policy evaluation with computable general equilibrium models

← Back to Research Areas

Overview

I've spent a good amount of time on economic policy modeling using computable general equilibrium modeling. The core concept there is that instead of using traditional macroeconomics tools, you create a network model of microeconomic relationships between different sectors in different regions and either solve for equilibrium conditions using a system of equations or simulate dynamic evolution using differential equations.

I don't have the expertise to independently develop novel models, but I have experience evaluating policy outcomes using these models while leveraging principles from decision-making under uncertainty. That combination matters more than it might sound: CGE results are highly sensitive to elasticity assumptions and baseline projections that nobody can pin down precisely, and reporting a single headline number from one parameterization gives a false impression of precision. Treating those assumptions as an uncertainty space to be explored, rather than inputs to be fixed, produces conclusions that survive contact with the real world.
